Carmen Iverson brought the core team together to kick off Dune and agree on its goal, its owner and its target launch date.

## Discussion

Carmen opened with the goal. Dune is the project to build a ceiling-mounted sensor for retrofit projects, and the product will be sold as the Larkspur Halo Gen 2. The point is to give building operators a sensor they can add to existing spaces without reworking the room, so ease of installation came up again and again during the meeting.

Rosa Novak raised the mechanical side: a ceiling mount brings its own constraints on the housing and on the way the sensor attaches. She asked for the enclosure requirements to be written early so that procurement can start talking to vendors. Ines Fitzgerald agreed to line up enclosure and battery vendors as soon as the requirements are drafted. Zoe Adeyemi and Hector Haddad went through the electronics and the connected side of the sensor, and both asked for a clear power target before they commit to an architecture.

The group then confirmed ownership. Carmen Iverson is the product owner of Dune, and she will write the requirements with input from each discipline. Questions on scope and priorities go to [[Carmen Iverson]], who owns the project from here to launch.

Finally, the team set the target date. Dune is scheduled to launch on May 4, 2026. Everyone agreed the date is ambitious but workable if the requirements and the vendor work start right away.

## Decisions

- Dune goes ahead as a ceiling-mounted sensor for retrofit projects, sold as the Larkspur Halo Gen 2.
- Carmen Iverson is the product owner.
- The target launch date is May 4, 2026.
