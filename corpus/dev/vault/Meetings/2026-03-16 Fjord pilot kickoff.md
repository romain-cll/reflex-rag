Diego Bishop and Claire Gallagher met to kick off the Fjord pilot. The purpose was to agree on the scope of the pilot and on how many units go to the customer.

## Discussion

Diego started with who the customer is. Whitmore Campus Services is piloting Fjord with 100 units. The customer's page, [[Whitmore Campus Services]], is the place to find the account background, and Diego asked that anyone dealing with the pilot read it first.

The two of them then talked about what a pilot is meant to show. The idea is to see the product run in real conditions, in the hands of a customer who does not know the design, and to collect feedback before the project goes further. Diego owns the pilot as part of the project, which is described on the page of [[Fjord]].

Claire raised the quality side. She wants every unit that leaves the building to be checked and recorded, so that any report from the customer can be matched to a specific unit. Diego agreed that traceability matters more than speed here.

They also talked about the tone of the pilot. It should feel like a partnership, with the customer free to say what works and what does not. Both of them expect the feedback to be useful even when it is critical.

## Decisions

- The pilot goes ahead with the customer and the number of units described above.
- Quality checks on the units are part of the pilot.
