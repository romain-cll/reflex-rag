Carmen Iverson and Naomi Boyle met to kick off the Dune pilot at Riverside Regional Hospital and pin down its scope and the number of units.

## Discussion

Carmen started with the customer. [[Riverside Regional Hospital]] is the hospital that agreed to host the Dune pilot, and its account page has the main contact and the background on the relationship. As with most hospital sites, access to clinical areas has to be planned with the facilities team in advance, so the installation schedule will follow their constraints rather than ours.

On scope, Riverside Regional Hospital is piloting Dune with 90 units. The units go into existing spaces, which is exactly the retrofit case the ceiling-mounted sensor was designed for, so the pilot should tell us a lot about real installation conditions. The pilot units will come from the DVT build.

Naomi covered the quality side. She wants every pilot unit tracked individually, with a simple way for the facilities staff to report anything that looks wrong, and a regular check-in to go through what comes back from the site. Carmen agreed and asked that findings from the pilot feed into the same issue list the engineering team already uses, so nothing gets lost between the pilot and the lab.

Background on the product, its owner and the requirements is on the [[Dune]] project page, which Carmen will point the hospital's team to if they ask.

## Decisions

- The pilot runs at Riverside Regional Hospital with 90 units.
- Pilot findings go into the shared engineering issue list.

## Action items

- Carmen to confirm installation access and timing with the hospital's facilities contact.
- Naomi to prepare a simple issue log and reporting route for the facilities staff.
- Naomi to label and track each pilot unit before it ships.
- Carmen to send a recap to the internal team.
