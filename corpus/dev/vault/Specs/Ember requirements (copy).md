These are the requirements of Ember, written right after [[2025-07-06 Ember kickoff]] while the discussion was still fresh. The project is owned by Hazel Osei as product owner. Every supplier offer for the project is checked against the sections below.

## Overview

Ember is a Larkspur sensor project, and since Larkspur outsources manufacturing, the requirements are written so they can be handed to suppliers as they stand. They cover four areas: power, cost, the enclosure and the circuit board assembly. The document is approved and serves as the reference for scope questions. Changes to a requirement go through the product owner.

## Power

The Ember requirements call for a battery life of 26 months. The electronics have to be designed around this requirement, from the sensing elements to the connectivity. A design choice that would shorten battery life is raised with the product owner before the team adopts it.

## Cost

The cost section holds the target unit cost agreed for Ember. Every supplier offer is read against that target. An offer that meets the technical needs but pushes the unit cost beyond the target goes back to the product owner, and procurement keeps the comparison current.

## Enclosure

The enclosure protects the electronics and has to hold up to the handling a sensor sees in everyday use. Mechanical engineering leads this section, starting with the choice of material and the shape of the housing. Tooling for the enclosure is requested from plastics vendors, and procurement gathers their offers so that price and delivery time can be compared side by side.

## Circuit board assembly

The circuit board carries the sensing and power electronics. Electrical engineering owns the board design, and a contract manufacturer assembles the boards, with pricing given per board. Procurement gathers the offers for the assembly and compares them with the cost target, as it does for the enclosure.
