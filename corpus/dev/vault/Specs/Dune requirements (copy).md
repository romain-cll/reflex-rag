## Overview

This document sets out the requirements for Dune. It was written right after [[2025-05-06 Dune kickoff]], while the discussion was still fresh, and it is meant to be the reference for engineering, procurement and quality as the design moves forward. Each section states what the product must achieve, and anyone working on a given area should check their choices against it.

## Power

The Dune requirements call for a battery life of 32 months. The monitor is meant to run unattended on its battery, so the electronics must be designed around a low average power draw. The sensing schedule and the radio activity are the main levers, and any change to either of them has to be checked against the battery life requirement before it is adopted.

## Cost

The unit cost target set for the project is the guide for every purchasing choice. The cost is that of the finished unit as delivered by the contract manufacturer, counting the enclosure, the battery pack, the electronics and assembly. Procurement compares each supplier offer with the cost target, and the product team reviews the running cost whenever a significant part is chosen.

## Enclosure

The enclosure protects the electronics, gives the sensor clean airflow and must be easy to mount where the monitor is used. It also has to withstand normal handling during installation and daily use. Because the enclosure needs tooling, procurement gathers offers from molding vendors and mechanical engineering checks each one against this section before the team compares them on price and delivery time.

## Battery pack

The battery pack must deliver the battery life set in the Power section at a price that fits the cost target. Procurement gathers offers for the pack, mechanical engineering confirms that it fits the enclosure, and quality reviews it before it is approved for the build. The pack should be easy for a technician to replace without special tools.
