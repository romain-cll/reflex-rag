This document lists the requirements for Fjord. It was written right after [[2025-09-07 Fjord kickoff]], while the discussion was fresh, and it is owned by Diego Bishop. Each section states what the design must meet. Offers from suppliers are gathered separately by procurement and are judged against these requirements.

## Overview

Fjord is a battery-powered sensor, and the requirements here cover the hardware as a whole: power, cost, the enclosure and the sensor module. When a requirement involves several disciplines, the product owner has the final say. This version is approved and serves as the reference for the design work that comes next.

## Power

The Fjord requirements call for a battery life of 34 months. The power design, including the choice of cells and the sleep behavior of the electronics, has to be judged against that figure. Any change that might affect battery life should go to the product owner before it enters the design.

## Cost

The finished unit has to come in at the cost target set for the product. That means the enclosure, the sensor module and everything else that goes into the box all count toward it. Each sourcing choice is checked against the target, and procurement flags any offer that puts it at risk.

## Enclosure

The enclosure protects the electronics and has to hold the board and the battery securely. It is made of tooled plastic parts produced by an outside molder, so this section asks each supplier for the cost of the tooling and for the time needed to deliver it. Procurement collects the offers and hardware engineering checks them against the mechanical needs described here.

## Sensor module

The sensor module is the part that measures the air, and it comes from an outside supplier. This section asks for a price per module, so that the module can be weighed against the cost target. Hardware engineering reviews each offer for fit with the board design before anything is agreed.
